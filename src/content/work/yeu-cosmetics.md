**The brief.** YÊU, a new science-backed, gender-neutral makeup brand, needed to enter a ₹34,000cr-by-2028 Indian beauty market where a share-of-voice chart showed Estée Lauder at 377 media mentions against YÊU's near-zero.

**The observation.** An overcrowded, trust-driven category doesn't reward louder shouting — it rewards a distinct emotional territory competitors haven't claimed. YÊU's "science-backed, for everyone" premise needed a cultural hook, not just product claims.

**The idea.** "Shades of YÊU" — an art x beauty campaign that treats every person as both an artist and a work of art, staged as a gallery-style exhibition/video series. Alongside it, "Make-up Dikhai": reframing the wedding "Muh Dikhai" ritual to give grooms a culturally-sanctioned space to get made up and talk openly about wedding anxieties — a first for the category.

**Execution.** A phased Awareness → Disruption → Outreach rollout, a media "Fam Trip" influencer seeding event, a full magazine/digital/podcast media plan (Vogue, Elle, Figuring Out with Raj Shamani), named talent recommendations (Vijay Varma, Tamannaah Bhatia) as brand faces, and a seasonal campaign calendar tying product drops to festive/wedding moments.

**Curator's note.** "Make-up Dikhai" is the idea I'd point to first — it takes an existing cultural ritual and finds the one unclaimed emotional gap inside it (male anxiety around weddings) rather than manufacturing a new occasion from scratch.
