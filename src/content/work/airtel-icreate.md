**The brief.** 100+ consumer interviews surfaced 5 barriers to telecom bundling adoption: bundling read as lock-in rather than convenience, a "hostage effect" where one broken service blocks unrelated cancellations, a punitive exit process, low trust in customer service, and a gap between the "frictionless" promise and reality.

**The observation.** India's smart-home market is projected at ₹36,000cr by 2028, and telcos are shifting from bandwidth providers to AI-driven platform players — the bundling complaint wasn't really about pricing, it was about telcos still selling disconnected services under one bill.

**The idea.** "Altura Home Intelligence" — an AI HomeOS that learns household routines, OTT preferences, and security needs, and self-manages bandwidth, family modes, subscriptions, and security as one product rather than a bundle.

**Execution.** A go-to-market split between acquisition (real-estate/builder partnerships, experience marketing, seamless activation) and expansion (AI household optimizer, retention via health reports), mapped across a 7-stage customer journey from discovery to predictive self-healing support.

**Curator's note.** The bundling problem looked like a pricing or churn problem until we mapped the actual emotional journey — it was a trust and control problem, which is why the fix had to be a product, not a discount.
