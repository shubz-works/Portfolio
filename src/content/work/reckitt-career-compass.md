**The brief.** A top Delhi Metro distributor showed every symptom of trouble at once — ₹1.8 Cr of excess stock against a 7-day norm, ₹1 Cr of outstanding market credit, a margin renegotiation demand, wholesale share creeping from 25% to 32% while retail declined, VI payouts collapsing, and rising salesforce attrition.

**The observation.** Seven data exhibits made clear the symptoms weren't independent — the wholesale creep was a salesforce incentive problem, not a demand problem, and the credit and stock issues were downstream of that same misalignment.

**The idea.** A weighted decision matrix (11 criteria, 100 points) scored Continue vs. Renegotiate vs. Replace — Renegotiate won clearly (775 vs. 669 vs. 419) — built on four moves: working capital optimization, tightening the credit cycle, salesforce revitalization, and rebalancing channel mix.

**Execution.** Resetting wholesale discounts to 2.5–3%, introducing retail-exclusive SKU packs, rationalizing the assortment from 120 to 85–90 SKUs, and redesigning the incentive scheme around Expectancy/Equity/Goal-Setting theory with a tiered Gold/Silver/Bronze structure — projected at a 1,293% training ROI.

**Curator's note.** The tempting answer was to replace the distributor — the numbers said the relationship was still worth more repaired than replaced, which is the harder recommendation to make convincingly.
