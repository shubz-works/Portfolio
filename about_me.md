## Patterns across the work

- **Consistent methodology across very different industries** (telecom, FMCG, luxury beauty, cab aggregation, housing finance, retail): primary research (interviews, street surveys) + secondary market sizing → problem diagnosis → phased GTM strategy → named campaign/big-idea → implementation roadmap with budgets, KPIs, and risk mitigation.
- **Comfortable across sectors**: telecom/IoT, FMCG sales & ESG, luxury cosmetics, ride-hailing/mobility, dermatology/baby care, B2B distributor economics, campus wellness tech, D2C fashion re-entry.
- **A recurring soft-power/emotional-marketing instinct**: several ideas (Britannia's gig-worker insurance, Yêu's "Make-up Dikhai" reimagining a wedding ritual for grooms, Cetaphil's "Authority Before Advocacy") reframe a cultural ritual or social-good angle into a brand mechanic, rather than leaning on pure product-benefit messaging.
- **Numbers-literate**: comfortable building weighted decision matrices, ROI/working-capital calculations, and quantified KPI targets (e.g., Reckitt's distributor ROI at 61.6%, MakeMyTrip's ₹100 Cr revenue target) — not just qualitative brand storytelling.
- **Agency-side depth**: the Adfactors decks reflect real commercial PR deliverables — agency credentials, actual client rosters, named internal leadership and account-team structures, and a live crisis-management case (the PNB Housing SEBI whistleblower story) — plus comfort running large-scale, multi-workstream retainer accounts (the PNB Housing deck alone runs 55 slides across FY25 recap, competitor tracking, regional expansion, HR/CSR, and 4+ named campaigns at once).
